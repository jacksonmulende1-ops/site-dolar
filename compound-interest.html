How Compound Interest Works

Compound interest is a way of calculating growth where interest can be earned on both the original amount of money and previously accumulated interest.

What Is Compound Interest?

Imagine you start with $100 and earn interest. If the interest remains in the account, the balance becomes larger. Future interest can then be calculated on this larger balance.

Why Time Matters

Time can have a significant effect on compound growth. The longer money remains invested or saved while earning a return, the more opportunities there are for growth to build on previous growth.

Regular Contributions

Adding money regularly can increase the amount available to grow. Even relatively small contributions may become more significant over a long period, depending on the interest or investment return.

Interest Rate Matters

A higher rate can lead to faster growth, while a lower rate can produce slower growth. However, higher potential returns may also involve higher risks.

Example

Suppose you start with $100 and earn a return over time. Instead of withdrawing the earnings, you leave them in the account. The balance can then grow as future returns are calculated on the larger amount.

Compound Interest and Debt

Compound interest is not only relevant to savings and investments. Some forms of debt can also grow because interest and fees increase the amount owed. This is why understanding interest rates is important before borrowing money.

Important Things to Consider

Before making financial decisions, consider fees, inflation, taxes, interest rates and investment risks. Actual results can differ from simple calculator estimates.

Final Thoughts

Compound interest can be powerful because growth can build on previous growth. Starting early, contributing consistently and understanding the risks can help you make more informed financial decisions.
